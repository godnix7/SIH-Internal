1. Executive Summary
1.1 What problem is being solved?
Tourists — especially solo travellers, women, foreigners, the elderly, and trekkers in remote regions — face a detection-and-coordination problem, not primarily an absence-of-emergency-services problem. India already operates a pan-India emergency number (ERSS-112) that receives distress signals over ten channels and dispatches police, health, and fire resources on a GIS map [VERIFIED — MHA/C-DAC, 112.gov.in]. What is missing is:
Nobody knows a tourist is in trouble until someone reports it. In the May 2025 Meghalaya honeymoon case, a couple vanished on 23 May; the husband's body was found only on 2 June — a 10-day detection gap in one of India's most-visited tourist corridors [VERIFIED — Wikipedia/press timeline].
Nobody knows where the tourist is once phones are off, out of network, or the person is off-route. In the June 2024 Sahastra Tal trek disaster, 9 of 22 trekkers died of hypothermia after losing the route in a blizzard; rescue helicopters were deployed only days after the group missed check-ins [VERIFIED — The Wire, Outlook, IAF statements].
Response is fragmented across agencies — police, tourism department, SDRF/NDRF, forest department, hospitals, and district administration each maintain separate records, verification steps, and jurisdictional boundaries, which slows the "golden hour."
Trust and record-integrity problems — tourists (especially foreigners) distrust local reporting processes; authorities face fake or exaggerated reports; families dispute official timelines; there is no shared tamper-evident log of who knew what, when.
1.2 Why is tourist safety monitoring hard despite smartphones and 112?
Connectivity is worst exactly where risk is highest (Himalayan treks, NE hill roads, forest zones, coastal dead zones). A cloud-first SOS app fails precisely when it is needed. [RESEARCH-BACKED]
Tourists are transient and anonymous. Police have no baseline: no itinerary, no expected return time, no emergency contacts, often no local SIM (foreigners). Missing-person detection therefore depends on family noticing silence from another state or country. [RESEARCH-BACKED]
Reporting friction: language barriers, fear of police interaction, unfamiliarity with local numbers (many foreigners do not know 112; 911/999 habits), and jurisdiction confusion. [RESEARCH-BACKED]
Response variance: even within ERSS-112, ground checks show response times ranging from ~4 minutes in cities to 50+ minutes in weaker pockets [VERIFIED — India TV field check, Mar 2026; MHA state ERSS rankings exist and are publicised, e.g., Himachal ranked fastest — Tribune, May 2026].
Privacy tension: continuous tracking of visitors by the state is legally and ethically sensitive, especially post-DPDP Act 2023 / DPDP Rules 2025 [VERIFIED — MeitY Gazette notification 13–14 Nov 2025].
1.3 Who benefits?
Tourists (faster help, proactive warnings, verified identity without carrying documents), families (visibility and notification), police & tourism departments (situational awareness, fewer blind searches, auditability), SDRF/NDRF and forest departments (pre-registered trekker manifests), hospitals (identity + emergency contacts + blood group on arrival), and state governments (tourism-brand protection — the April 2025 Pahalgam attack on tourists caused an immediate, severe collapse in Kashmir tourist arrivals, demonstrating how a single incident destroys a destination economy) [RESEARCH-BACKED — widely reported; not re-verified in this pass].
1.4 What must the system fundamentally achieve?
Reduce time-to-detection and time-to-verified-response for tourist incidents, with consent, in low-connectivity conditions, across agency boundaries — while creating records all parties can trust.
1.5 Problem definition
One sentence: Tourists in India remain vulnerable because no system knows who they are, where they intended to be, or that they are in trouble until it is too late — and the agencies that respond cannot coordinate on shared, trustworthy, real-time information.
One paragraph: India's emergency infrastructure (ERSS-112, SDRF, tourist police units) is reactive: it activates only after a call is made, yet the highest-fatality tourist incidents — trekkers lost in weather, travellers missing in remote corridors, victims in no-network zones — are precisely those where no call ever comes. Tourists carry no machine-readable identity or itinerary that responders can use; families detect disappearance days late; agencies coordinate over phone calls and paper; and incident records are contested. The proposed system closes this loop with (a) an opt-in digital tourist ID carrying itinerary and emergency contacts, (b) consent-based location monitoring with geo-fence warnings and missed-check-in/anomaly detection that works degraded-offline, and (c) an authority dashboard with auditable, tamper-evident incident logs shared across police, tourism, disaster-management, and health agencies.
Detailed form: See Sections 2, 8, and 18. In short, the product's real job is: pre-registration of trip context → passive risk-aware monitoring → earliest-possible anomaly/SOS signal → verified dispatch with full context → multi-agency coordinated response → trusted post-incident record. Everything else (scores, heatmaps, wearables, blockchain) is subordinate to that chain and must be justified against it.

2. Tourist Safety Problem Analysis
India recorded roughly 2.19 billion domestic tourist visits (2023, Ministry of Tourism figures widely cited) and ~9.2–9.5 million foreign tourist arrivals annually in recent years [RESEARCH-BACKED — Ministry of Tourism "India Tourism Statistics"; exact figures should be re-pulled from tourism.gov.in for the final PRD]. Even very low per-capita incident rates therefore translate into thousands of emergencies per year.
A key statistical caveat [RESEARCH-BACKED]: NCRB's "Crime against Foreigners" chapter records only a few hundred registered cases per year — 257 in 2024, up 8% from 238 in 2023, with theft the most common offence [VERIFIED — NCRB Crime in India 2024, released May 2026]. These figures are widely believed to understate reality due to under-reporting (tourists leave before filing FIRs, language barriers, reluctance). Product decisions should treat official crime data as a floor, not a measure of true incidence. Domestic-tourist incidents are not separately tracked at all in national statistics — itself a data gap the system could help close.

3. Real-World Incident Research
Cases below were selected because they are well-documented and each isolates a different failure mode. Technology counterfactuals are stated honestly, including where the proposed stack would not have helped.
3.1 Sahastra Tal trek disaster — Uttarkashi, Uttarakhand, June 2024 [VERIFIED]
What happened: A 22-member Karnataka Mountaineering Association group began the ~35 km Sahastra Tal trek on 29 May 2024; after summiting (~4,600 m), a blizzard on descent caused the group to lose the route. Nine trekkers, including a 71-year-old woman, died of hypothermia. IAF Cheetah and Mi-17 helicopters plus SDRF evacuated 13 survivors on 5–6 June.
What failed: No live position telemetry; alerting depended on the group being overdue against an informal return date (7 June); weather window not enforced; the group's registration data did not translate into active monitoring.
Detection delay? Yes — distress began ~2–3 June; large-scale rescue on 5–6 June.
Location unavailable? Yes — searchers had to locate the party visually by helicopter.
Could geo-fencing have helped? Partially — a defined trek corridor with "deviation beyond X m" logic would flag route loss, but only if positions could reach a server. In a Himalayan dead zone, this requires offline buffering + satellite/mesh/SMS fallback, or checkpoint-based (not continuous) monitoring. Honest answer: corridor geo-fencing plus mandatory checkpoint check-ins with automatic escalation on miss is the realistic mechanism; pure GPS-to-cloud geofencing would not have worked live.
Could earlier risk detection have helped? Yes — weather-conditional route closure (IMD feed → zone status) could have blocked the summit window.
Could coordination tech have helped? Moderately — SDRF/IAF/district coordination eventually worked well; the loss was in detection, not dispatch.
Tamper-proof records? Not a factor here.
3.2 Raja Raghuvanshi case — Sohra (Cherrapunji), Meghalaya, May–June 2025 [VERIFIED]
What happened: A honeymooning couple from Indore checked out of a Nongriat homestay on 23 May 2025 and vanished; last phone contact ~1:43 PM that day, then both phones off. Family filed a missing report the same day. A rented scooter was found abandoned. The husband's body was found in a gorge below Wei Sawdong Falls on 2 June — ten days later — after a large multi-agency search (police, NDRF). The case became a homicide investigation; the wife was arrested on 9 June, and in 2026 the case produced a procedural-lapse bail controversy now before the Supreme Court.
What failed: (a) Detection: despite a same-day missing report, there was no last-known-location trail, no itinerary of planned stops, no homestay-linked check-out/check-in chain, so searchers combed an enormous terrain blind. (b) Record integrity: the later bail on arrest-procedure grounds illustrates how contested process records can undermine even strong cases.
Could geo-fencing have helped? Marginally as fences per se; enormously as breadcrumb location history — even a coarse, battery-light location trail with the tourist's consent would have narrowed the search from ten days of open-terrain combing.
Could tamper-proof incident records have helped? Plausibly at the margins — an append-only, timestamped log of arrest/search/notification events (grounds-of-arrest served, remand steps) is exactly the artefact courts examined. [ASSUMPTION — whether digital logs would satisfy the specific legal standard requires legal validation.]
Sober caveat: this was a premeditated murder; the perpetrators would have disabled tracking. Tech's realistic value here is search-space reduction and process integrity, not prevention.
3.3 Pahalgam terror attack — Baisaran, J&K, 22 April 2025 [RESEARCH-BACKED — extensively reported; details below reflect mainstream reporting and should be re-verified before external publication]
What happened: Militants attacked tourists at the Baisaran meadow near Pahalgam, killing 26 people, almost all tourists. The meadow is reachable only by foot/pony; response was delayed by terrain. The attack triggered a mass exodus of tourists from Kashmir and severe geopolitical escalation.
What failed for tourists as a class: no rapid means to (a) establish who was in the zone (families spent hours confirming whereabouts), (b) push evacuation/avoidance alerts to tourists en route, (c) coordinate casualty identification.
Could this system have helped? Honestly: it could not have prevented the attack. Value would be post-event: zone roll-call ("N registered tourists inside fence — M confirmed safe"), family notification, casualty identification via digital ID, and immediate temporary exclusion fencing for inbound tourists. This is the strongest real-world argument for the "who is inside this polygon right now" capability — and also the most privacy-sensitive one.
3.4 Kedarnath flash floods, Uttarakhand, June 2013 [RESEARCH-BACKED]
Thousands of pilgrims and tourists died or went missing when flash floods destroyed the valley. Post-disaster, authorities could not even establish how many visitors were present. This event directly motivated Uttarakhand's later mandatory Char Dham yatra registration system [RESEARCH-BACKED]. Lesson: visitor manifests per risk zone are a proven, already-adopted government response — the proposed system generalises it.
3.5 Kerala floods, August 2018 [RESEARCH-BACKED]
State-wide flooding stranded large numbers of domestic and foreign tourists (notably in Munnar and Kochi); airport closure complicated exits. Coordination between tourism dept, district administration and rescue worked comparatively well (Kerala's disaster machinery is strong), but tourist-specific tracking did not exist; hotels became the de facto manifest. Lesson: hotels/homestays are a critical data node — check-in data is the state's best proxy for tourist location. (Foreigner check-ins are already reported via Form-C to the Bureau of Immigration [RESEARCH-BACKED].)
3.6 Crimes against women travellers (pattern, not single case) [VERIFIED baseline data]
NCRB 2022: 25 rapes of foreign nationals registered; Karnataka, Himachal, Maharashtra led state counts of crimes against foreigners; the March 2024 Dumka (Jharkhand) assault on a Spanish travel blogger drew international attention [VERIFIED — ETV Bharat/NCRB]. Failure modes: victims did not know local emergency channels; incidents in low-witness rural areas; reporting friction. Realistic tech value: discreet SOS, live trusted-contact sharing, night-risk-zone advisories, multilingual reporting. Tech cannot substitute for policing and prosecution.

5. User Personas
P1 — Arjun, 27, solo backpacker (Bengaluru → Spiti). Tech-savvy engineer, travels alone on long weekends, phone is his lifeline. Goals: freedom without worrying his parents. Pain: long no-network stretches; parents panic. Expects: set-and-forget monitoring, auto "I'm fine" pings to family, offline maps, SOS that works on the power button. Emergency expectation: if he misses two check-ins, someone competent looks at his last-known location — without spamming police for every dead battery.
P2 — Elena, 34, international tourist (Spain → Rajasthan/Himachal). Second visit to India; no local SIM for first 48h; doesn't know 112. Goals: authentic travel, document safety. Pain: language, permit confusion, fear from news coverage of assaults on foreign women. Expects: English-first app offered at airport immigration with her digital ID; embassy contact one tap away; verified guides; works over Wi-Fi. Emergency expectation: an operator who speaks English and already knows who and where she is.
P3 — Priya, 29, woman travelling for work + leisure (Delhi → Guwahati → Shillong). Goals: evening freedom in unfamiliar cities. Pain: harassment risk, unreliable cabs at night, doesn't want visible panic apps. Expects: silent SOS (volume-button pattern), live trip-share to her sister with auto-alarm if the cab deviates from route, night-risk-zone nudges phrased respectfully, option to reach a woman officer. Emergency expectation: acknowledgment within seconds, not a call-back queue.
P4 — Ramesh, 46, trek leader with a 14-member group (Sahastra Tal-type route). Moderate tech ability; carries power banks; knows the dead zones. Goals: bring everyone back; keep his operator licence. Pain: no way to signal distress above the last village; registration paperwork that never helps him. Expects: group manifest linking, checkpoint QR/beacon check-ins that buffer offline and sync at the next signal pocket, weather-closure notices before the trailhead, and a protocol where a missed checkpoint triggers graduated escalation (call attempts → local forest post → SDRF). Emergency expectation: rescuers get his route plan and last checkpoint automatically.
P5 — Susan, 68, elderly tourist on a group tour (UK → Kerala). Low tech ability; hypertension; iPhone with large text. Goals: comfort and health security. Expects: one giant HELP button; her medical card (blood group, medications, GP contact, travel insurance) reaches the ambulance; her daughter in London is notified automatically. Emergency expectation: she never has to explain anything twice.
P6 — SI Dorjee, 38, police sub-inspector, tourist-heavy hill district. Handles 112 dispatches plus local complaints; short-staffed; two constables per shift. Goals: clear alerts fast, zero missed genuine SOS, minimal paperwork. Expects: a triaged queue (verified-ID SOS ≠ anonymous ping), one-tap acknowledge/dispatch, last-known-location + itinerary on the incident card, auto-generated case log he can attach to the FIR. Fear: being disciplined because a dashboard timestamp proves he acknowledged late — the system must be fair and account for ground reality, or officers will resist it. P7 — Meera, 41, SDRF response-team lead. Goals: shrink search boxes. 6. Complete User Journeys
Format: Trigger → User action → System expectation → Authority action → Failure scenarios → Desired outcome.
J1. Tourist registration / digital ID issuance. Trigger: arrival at airport/hotel/check-post or self-serve in app. User: submits KYC (Aadhaar via DigiLocker for domestic; passport+visa scan for foreigners), trip dates, itinerary, emergency contacts; grants granular consents. System: identity verification, issues time-bound digital ID (valid only for trip duration, auto-expiring) as a verifiable credential + QR; consent receipt stored. Authority: none in the happy path; tourism dept sees aggregate counts only. Failures: KYC API downtime (→ provisional ID with deferred verification); foreigner without connectivity (→ kiosk/hotel-assisted issuance); consent screens skipped-through (→ layered consent, plain language, DPDP-compliant itemised notice [VERIFIED requirement — DPDP Rules 2025]). Outcome: a tourist exists in the system with context and revocable consent.
J2. Trip creation & starting monitoring. Trigger: user starts a trip/leg. User: selects destinations/route, monitoring level (Off / Check-ins only / Geo-fence alerts / Full tracking), family-share preferences. System: downloads offline zone pack + maps for the region; schedules check-ins; battery-aware sampling. Failures: user forgets to start (→ geo-detected "you seem to be travelling — start trip?" nudge, opt-in); over-broad default consent (→ default to the least invasive level) [RECOMMENDATION]. Outcome: monitoring active at the level the tourist chose, and they can state that level back correctly (comprehension test in UX research).
J3. Background monitoring (steady state). System: low-power location sampling; on-device geo-fence evaluation against cached polygons; queue-and-forward when offline; daily auto "OK" to family if enabled. Failures: OS kills background app (Android OEM battery managers are notorious) [RESEARCH-BACKED]; battery anxiety → uninstall; GPS drift in gorges/urban canyons (→ require N consecutive fixes / accuracy threshold before any zone event). Outcome: invisible, boring, battery-cheap.
J4. Entering a risky area. Trigger: on-device fence-entry event (advisory-class zone). User: receives calm, specific notification in their language ("Petty theft reported frequently here after 21:00; keep valuables secured; nearest police aid post 400 m"). System: logs event locally; no authority notification for advisory zones [RECOMMENDATION — privacy]. Failures: alert fatigue from over-fencing (→ strict zone-quality governance, cap advisories/day); GPS error at boundary (→ hysteresis buffer). Outcome: informed, not policed.
J5. Entering a restricted zone (border/military/forest core). Trigger: fence entry, restricted-class. User: strong warning + guidance to exit + legal note. System: if dwell exceeds threshold, notifies the owning authority (forest dept / police) with ID + location per the disclosed policy. Authority: contacts/intercepts per SOP. Failures: false entry from GPS error near boundary (→ buffer zones, dwell-time and accuracy gates before escalation — a wrongful stop of a foreign tourist is a diplomatic incident); genuine permit-holders flagged (→ permit linked to ID suppresses alert). Outcome: prevention first, enforcement second.
J6. Trek route deviation / missed checkpoint. Trigger: device >X m off corridor for >T min, or checkpoint missed by >T. User: on-device alarm "You appear off-route; backtrack guidance"; one-tap "I'm fine / intentional detour". System: if unacknowledged and offline, phone enters beacon mode (periodic SMS attempts with compressed coords if any signal; louder local alarm); server escalates on checkpoint-miss timeout using last synced data. Authority: graduated — trek operator → forest post → district control → SDRF. Failures: everyone is offline (accept: server-side detection then depends on absence of expected sync — design the escalation around missed expectations, not live data); false alarms from planned detours (→ group-lead override). Outcome: Sahastra Tal-type events detected in hours, not days.
J7. Receiving vs ignoring a warning. Ignored advisory: system does nothing further except log locally (respect autonomy) [RECOMMENDATION]. Ignored restricted/disaster warning: escalate per J5/J8. Failure: warnings so frequent they're dismissed reflexively — measured via acknowledgment-rate KPI (§17).
J8. Disaster zone activation (flood/landslide/security). Trigger: SDMA/police declare a temporary emergency polygon. System: push + SMS + (where integrated) Cell Broadcast to devices in/near polygon, multilingual; roll-call requests ("Mark yourself safe"); exit routing. Authority: dashboard shows inside-count, safe-marked, unresponsive list with last fixes prioritised for SAR. Failures: network destroyed by the same disaster (→ Cell Broadcast/SACHET integration is the resilient channel [RESEARCH-BACKED — NDMA CAP-based SACHET exists]; app is secondary); panic from badly worded alerts (→ pre-approved templates). Outcome: Kedarnath/Kerala-class events get a live manifest instead of guesswork.
J9. Manual SOS. Trigger: user taps SOS / power-button pattern / SHOUT-style trigger. User: optionally selects type (police/medical/silent). System: <5 s to transmit ID, live location, medical card, itinerary to control; starts live tracking + audio capture per consent; simultaneously notifies emergency contacts; if offline → SMS fallback to 112-linked shortcode; UI shows "acknowledged by [unit] at [time]". Authority: call-taker sees a verified-identity incident (triage priority above anonymous calls) [RECOMMENDATION]; dispatch per ERSS SOP; two-way call-back. Failures: pocket triggers (→ 5-second cancel window with PIN); no ack within N seconds (→ auto-retry alternate channel + escalate to supervisor queue). Outcome: seconds to acknowledged, minutes to dispatched.
J10. Automatic risk detection (anomaly). Trigger: prolonged inactivity vs. itinerary, phone dark mid-trip in a risk zone, possible-crash signature, route deviation at night. System: always challenge the user first ("Are you OK? Respond within 10 min") before contacting anyone [RECOMMENDATION — false-positive control]; then notify family; only then authorities per severity matrix. Failures: dead battery ≠ emergency (→ severity matrix weighs zone risk + itinerary + time); over-escalation destroys police trust in the system permanently. Outcome: the Raghuvanshi-type silence is flagged within hours with a search box measured in hundreds of metres, not districts.
J11. Incident reporting (non-emergency) & e-FIR. Trigger: theft/harassment after the fact. User: files structured report in own language, attaches photos, gets a stamped acknowledgment. System: routes to jurisdictional PS; generates e-FIR draft where state rules allow (missing-person e-FIR auto-filing is explicitly envisioned in the PS) [VERIFIED — SIH25002 text]; hash-anchors the submission (§12). Authority: verify, register, update status visible to the tourist even after they leave the state/country. Failures: jurisdiction ping-pong (→ zero-FIR principle in workflow) [RESEARCH-BACKED — Indian law permits Zero FIR; validate operational integration]. Outcome: reporting a theft takes 10 minutes, not a day.
J12. Police response → hospital handoff → family notification → resolution. System maintains one incident timeline: SOS 14:02 → ack 14:03 → unit dispatched 14:06 → on-scene 14:19 → ambulance 108 handoff 14:31 (medical card transferred) → hospital admit 14:58 (access logged) → family notified 14:35 → incident closed with disposition code; tourist gets a closure summary + feedback prompt; record hash anchored. Failures at each seam are the product's core reliability work (dropped handoffs, duplicate incidents from same event via app+112 call → dedup logic).
J13. Post-incident follow-up. Closure survey; consular notification workflow for foreigners [OPEN QUESTION — MEA/embassy protocol integration]; insurance certificate issuance on tourist request; data deletion per retention schedule after trip end (see §14).

7.1 Landscape
Government / India
112 India app (ERSS) [VERIFIED — 112.gov.in]: pan-India SOS to state Emergency Response Centres over voice/SMS/SOS/app/WhatsApp etc.; SHOUT feature alerts nearby registered volunteers for women/children; power-button SOS trigger; location auto-forwarded to state control room on a GIS map. State tourism & police apps (e.g., Goa Tourism, Kerala Police "Pol-App", Delhi Police Himmat Plus for women) [RESEARCH-BACKED]: fragmented per state; SOS + info features; adoption among actual tourists is low; no interoperability across states — a tourist crossing Delhi→Himachal→Uttarakhand would need three ecosystems.
Incredible India / 1363 multilingual helpline [RESEARCH-BACKED]: information & grievance, not response.
International
Japan "Safety tips" app (JNTO/JMA) [RESEARCH-BACKED]: multilingual disaster alerts (earthquake early warning, tsunami, weather) push-targeted to foreign visitors. Gold standard for multilingual hazard alerting; no tracking, no SOS dispatch.
South Korea 1330 / Emergency Ready app [RESEARCH-BACKED]: 24/7 multilingual tourist helpline + disaster guidance.
Smart Traveler (US State Dept) / STEP enrolment [RESEARCH-BACKED]: itinerary enrolment so embassies can find citizens in a crisis — validates the "registered itinerary" concept at national scale.
Dubai / Singapore smart-city policing [RESEARCH-BACKED]: dense CCTV+analytics; effective but rests on surveillance norms India's framework does not replicate for visitors.
Private / consumer
Garmin inReach / ZOLEO / iPhone-Android satellite SOS [RESEARCH-BACKED]: satellite SOS + breadcrumb tracking; the only products that truly solve the no-network trek problem today; hardware cost ₹25k+ plus subscription; iPhone 14+ / recent Android satellite SOS availability in India is expanding but carrier/regulatory status must be re-verified [OPEN QUESTION].
Life360 / Google family sharing / WhatsApp live location [RESEARCH-BACKED]: family visibility; no authority link; continuous-tracking battery cost; privacy model is family-social, not emergency-institutional.
bSafe, Noonlight (US), Flare [RESEARCH-BACKED]: personal-safety SOS with monitoring centres (Noonlight bridges to 911 with verified data — the closest analogue to this system's SOS-with-context concept, but US-only).
TripWhistle, GeoSure, Sitata [RESEARCH-BACKED]: destination risk scores and advisories; information-only.
7.2 Comparison matrix
Competitive conclusion [RECOMMENDATION]: No existing product combines verified identity + itinerary + consented monitoring + authority dispatch integration + multi-agency record. The strategic posture must be "the tourist-context layer on top of ERSS-112 and SACHET" — never a parallel emergency system. Building a second dispatch pipeline would fragment response and be rejected by MHA/state police.

8. Gap Analysis

9. Emergency Response Workflow Research
9.1 How response actually works in India today [VERIFIED core / RESEARCH-BACKED details]
ERSS-112 pipeline (MHA, built by C-DAC as Total Service Provider): distress signals arrive at the State Public Safety Answering Point (PSAP) over ten channels — voice, SMS, app SOS, email, web, chatbot, WhatsApp, IoT signals, panic buttons, media crawler [VERIFIED — erss/MHA]. A Call-Taker validates the signal (filtering the large non-actionable share), captures/auto-fetches location onto a GIS map, classifies (police/fire/health/disaster/women/child/railways), and forwards the incident record to a Dispatcher, who assigns the nearest Emergency Response Vehicle via Computer-Aided Dispatch; vehicles carry Mobile Data Terminals and are live-tracked; escalation runs through shift supervisors and district control rooms; caller feedback may be collected post-incident [VERIFIED — MHA/C-DAC descriptions; India TV field observation of UP's 6,278-vehicle network].
Key workflow facts for integration design:
First alert receiver: state PSAP call-taker — therefore the proposed system's SOS must land as a structured ERSS signal (it already supports "IoT/external signals" channel), not as a phone call from a private NOC [VERIFIED channel exists; integration modality OPEN QUESTION per state].
Validation: human call-taker; a verified-ID + location + itinerary packet materially shortens this step [ASSUMPTION — quantify in pilot].
Assignment/escalation: dispatcher → ERV → supervisor → district control; disasters escalate to DM (incident commander under the Disaster Management Act 2005 structure) and SDMA/SDRF; NDRF on state request [RESEARCH-BACKED].
Missing persons: FIR/complaint at jurisdictional PS (Zero FIR possible anywhere) → entry in CCTNS → search coordination is manual; the PS text explicitly asks for automated e-FIR for missing persons [VERIFIED — SIH25002].
Medical: 108 ambulance services are separate state contracts (GVK EMRI et al. historically) being progressively unified under 112 [RESEARCH-BACKED — verify per pilot state].
False reporting: filtered at call-taking; repeat hoax numbers can face action under BNS provisions for false information [RESEARCH-BACKED — legal validation needed].
Closure/records: CAD event logs at PSAP; FIR/case diary in CCTNS for criminal matters; these are separate silos — the shared-timeline gap is real.
9.2 Realistic integration points [RECOMMENDATION]
SOS ingestion: deliver app SOS into the state PSAP as a pre-verified external signal with the full context packet. This is the highest-value, most feasible integration.
CAD status backhaul: subscribe to ack/dispatch/on-scene status so the tourist and family see progress (needs per-state MoU; technically supported by C-DAC stack) [OPEN QUESTION].
e-FIR/CCTNS: file structured missing-person/theft reports into CCTNS where the state permits digital intake.
SACHET/CAP: consume disaster alerts to auto-create temporary zones; contribute nothing (one-way).
108/hospital: emergency medical-card handoff via QR at patient side, avoiding deep HIS integration in v1.
What NOT to build: an independent dispatch/control room. [RECOMMENDATION — firm.]

10. Geo-Fencing Use-Case Research
Technical ground truth [RESEARCH-BACKED]: smartphone GNSS accuracy is ~3–5 m open-sky but degrades to 20–100 m+ in gorges, forests, and urban canyons; OS-level geofencing APIs (Android Geofencing API / iOS region monitoring) are power-efficient but latency-y (can take minutes and hundreds of metres to fire) and are throttled by OEM battery managers. Design consequence: every fence needs class-specific buffer, dwell-time, and accuracy-gating rules; and fence evaluation must run on-device against cached polygons so it works offline.
Zone governance is the hidden product [RECOMMENDATION]: who may author, who must approve, mandatory expiry for temporary zones, public transparency of advisory zones (but not necessarily restricted-zone precise geometry), and an audit trail of zone changes. Bad zones (stale, political, over-broad) will destroy user trust faster than any technical failure. [OPEN QUESTION: will police departments accept publishing "unsafe area" labels with tourism-economy consequences? Expect resistance; consider neutral phrasing — "stay-alert zones".]

11. AI Use-Case Validation
Principle applied: rules first; ML only where rules demonstrably fail. For an emergency system, explainability and predictable failure modes outweigh model sophistication.
Final AI judgment [RECOMMENDATION]: AI is justified in exactly three places for early versions — (1) multilingual translation/assistance, (2) assistive triage suggestions for human call-takers, (3) anomaly detection as a v2 refinement over a rules baseline. Everything else marketed as "AI" in this domain is either rules in a trench coat or an ethics problem.

12. Blockchain Use-Case Validation
Framework: blockchain earns its complexity only when (a) multiple parties who do not fully trust each other must (b) share a record that (c) none of them should be able to alter unilaterally, and (d) an ordinary database with an independent auditor is insufficient.
Final blockchain judgment [RECOMMENDATION]:
Do not put identity or location on a blockchain. It conflicts with DPDP erasure rights (immutability vs. right to erasure), creates a honeypot, and adds zero verification value over signed credentials. [RESEARCH-BACKED tension — legal validation advised.]
The defensible use is tamper-evidence for incident/audit records, implemented as: append-only hash-chained event log → Merkle roots anchored periodically to a permissioned consortium ledger (if ≥3 agencies commit to running nodes) or to a published transparency log (if they don't). Design the product so this layer is swappable.
For a hackathon/demo (§16), a minimal permissioned chain (e.g., 2–3 node Fabric/Besu) anchoring incident hashes demonstrates the concept honestly without pretending PII lives on-chain.
If stakeholders cannot name the distrusting parties who will operate nodes, blockchain should be dropped from production scope without embarrassment — the PS's underlying need is trustworthy records, and that need is met either way.

14. Legal & Regulatory Research
Disclaimer: the following is research orientation, not legal advice; every item requires validation by qualified counsel before implementation.
DPDP Act 2023 + DPDP Rules 2025 [VERIFIED]: Rules notified 13–14 Nov 2025 (Gazette G.S.R. 846(E)); phased commencement — Data Protection Board immediate, consent managers by Nov 2026, full substantive compliance by 13 May 2027. Requirements directly shaping this product: itemised plain-language notices; free/specific/informed/unconditional consent with easy withdrawal; purpose-bound retention and erasure; 72-hour breach reporting to the Board and affected individuals; verifiable parental consent and a prohibition on tracking/monitoring children; Significant Data Fiduciary duties (annual DPIA, audit, algorithmic assessment, DPO) which this system would almost certainly attract; State processing standards under the Second Schedule for government service delivery; liberalised cross-border transfer subject to government orders; penalties up to ₹250 crore. Timing note: a system launching 2026–27 must be DPDP-native from day one.
Section 7 / legitimate-uses and emergency processing [RESEARCH-BACKED]: the DPDP framework contemplates processing without fresh consent for responding to medical emergencies, disasters, and for State functions — the exact perimeter for "we tracked a non-responding tourist during an SOS" must be mapped by counsel.
Puttaswamy proportionality [VERIFIED precedent]: any state-mandated tracking must be necessary, proportionate, and least-intrusive; this is the constitutional argument for consent tiers and against mandatory enrolment.
Digital evidence: Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023 (s.63 certificate regime for electronic records, replacing Evidence Act s.65B) governs whether app logs/hash anchors are admissible; chain-of-custody design should target BSA compliance [RESEARCH-BACKED — legal validation essential].
Criminal procedure: BNSS 2023 formalises e-FIR/electronic complaint intake and Zero FIR; the automated missing-person e-FIR in the PS must map to BNSS provisions and state police SOPs [RESEARCH-BACKED].
Aadhaar Act & KYC: Aadhaar-based ID issuance for domestic tourists implicates the Aadhaar Act's authentication/offline-verification regimes (DigiLocker/offline XML paths are the low-friction route) [RESEARCH-BACKED].
Foreigners' regime: passport/visa verification, Form-C hotel reporting to FRRO/Bureau of Immigration, and ILP (Inner Line Permit) states (Arunachal, Mizoram, Nagaland, Manipur) plus PAP/RAP for foreigners in protected areas — the NE focus of the sponsoring ministry makes permit-aware geofencing legally load-bearing [RESEARCH-BACKED].
Disaster Management Act 2005: NDMA/SDMA/DM powers to direct, warn, and requisition during declared emergencies — the legal basis for temporary emergency zones and override alerts [RESEARCH-BACKED].
Telecom/alerting: Cell Broadcast (SACHET/CAP-India) is government-operated; integration requires NDMA/DoT arrangements, not private API keys [RESEARCH-BACKED].
Medical data: emergency medical-card sharing intersects DPDP plus health-sector norms (ABDM consent framework if integrating with health records) [OPEN QUESTION — keep v1 to self-declared medical cards to avoid ABDM dependency].
Liability [OPEN QUESTION — top-tier]: if the system fails to detect or a responder ignores an alert, who is liable? Terms must be written with counsel; over-promising "safety" invites litigation and moral hazard.

16. MVP Definition (hackathon / first prototype)
Scope philosophy: demonstrate the chain — register → monitor → detect → alert → respond → record — end to end, thin.
Roles (3): Tourist (mobile app), Authority operator (web dashboard), Admin (zone/user management). Family can be simulated via SMS/notification stub.
Workflows (5): (1) ID issuance with mock-KYC + QR credential; (2) trip creation with consent tiers; (3) geo-fence entry alert (advisory + restricted classes, on-device evaluation, simulated GPS paths for demo); (4) SOS → dashboard incident card (ID, location, medical card, itinerary) → operator acknowledge/dispatch/close with live status back to the app; (5) missed check-in → user-challenge → auto-escalation to dashboard.
Minimum safety features: SOS (tap + long-press), offline queue with SMS-fallback simulation, family notification message.
Minimum AI: rules-based anomaly engine (deviation distance / missed check-in / inactivity) + translation of alerts into 3–4 languages via an existing API; optionally an LLM-assisted incident summariser for the operator. Do not train models for the demo.
Minimum geo-fencing: polygon authoring on the dashboard map; on-device point-in-polygon with buffer + dwell logic; one restricted, one advisory, one temporary disaster zone in the demo script.
Minimum blockchain: hash-chain every incident event; anchor Merkle root to a 2-node permissioned chain (or a signed transparency log) and show a "verify record integrity" button that detects a tampered event. This demonstrates the honest use-case in minutes.
Mock, don't build: KYC (fake Aadhaar/passport verifier), ERSS/CCTNS integration (simulated PSAP endpoint), SMS gateway (log console), weather feed (canned), hospitals (QR scan demo page).
Do not attempt: real 112 integration, satellite comms, wearables, CCTV/face recognition, ML training, multi-state federation, real e-FIR.
Demo narrative: replay a Sahastra-Tal-style scenario on a map — trekker goes off-corridor offline → misses checkpoint → server escalates on missed sync → dashboard shows last-known fix + corridor → SDRF "dispatch" → record integrity verified. This maps the demo directly to a real, cited fatality case, which is far more persuasive than feature tours.

18. Final Recommendations
Top 10 validated problems: (1) detection gap for silent distress in remote areas [Sahastra Tal, Raghuvanshi — VERIFIED]; (2) absent last-known-location trails for search; (3) offline dead zones exactly where risk peaks; (4) no visitor manifest inside hazard zones [Kedarnath→Char Dham registration proves government demand]; (5) multi-agency coordination without a shared incident record; (6) response-time variance and hoax burden at PSAPs [VERIFIED spread]; (7) language barriers multiplying every other failure; (8) reporting friction and FIR abandonment by transient tourists [NCRB undercount pattern]; (9) contested incident/process records [Raghuvanshi bail — VERIFIED]; (10) fragmented per-state apps with zero interoperability.
Top 10 recommended features: consent-tiered monitoring with auto-expiry; time-bound verifiable digital ID; context-rich SOS into ERSS PSAPs; offline-first fence/check-in engine with SMS fallback; missed-check-in escalation with user-challenge; authority incident dashboard with dedup + status backhaul; disaster-zone roll-call; trek corridor + checkpoint module; multilingual alert/translation layer; tamper-evident hash-anchored incident logging.
Top 5 major risks: (1) governance vacuum — no agency owns operation, product dies in pilot; (2) privacy backlash / one misuse story ends adoption; (3) false-positive fatigue causing police to ignore the channel; (4) offline problem underestimated — system demos well in cities, fails on the mountain; (5) legal exposure over liability for missed detections and DPDP non-compliance (full compliance deadline 13 May 2027 [VERIFIED]).
Top 5 assumptions: (1) tourists will opt in if value and privacy tiers are clear (~30%+ at entry points); (2) at least one state's police/tourism dept will sign an ERSS-ingestion MoU; (3) verified-ID SOS genuinely shortens PSAP triage (quantify in pilot); (4) checkpoint-based escalation is operationally acceptable to SDRF/forest departments; (5) hotels/operators will participate in itinerary confirmation.
Top 5 unresolved questions: (1) Which agency owns and operates the platform (tourism vs police vs SDMA vs joint cell)? (2) What is the lawful perimeter of emergency location processing without fresh consent under DPDP §7? (3) Will any consortium of agencies actually run blockchain nodes — if not, transparency-log fallback? (4) Will states permit publishing risk-zone advisories with tourism-economy implications? (5) What liability regime applies when detection fails?
Recommended MVP: as §16 — the thin end-to-end chain, demoed against the Sahastra Tal scenario.
Recommended future scope: satellite/mesh comms for trek corridors, cross-state federation toward a national layer, crowd-risk analytics for mega-events, ABDM/insurance integrations.
Avoid: person-scoring, facial recognition, PII-on-chain, parallel dispatch, auto-dispatch AI, mandatory enrolment, fake-report classifiers, ambient audio/video.
Is AI justified? Qualified yes. Indispensable for multilingual assistance; valuable as assistive triage and (v2) anomaly refinement; unjustified — and hazardous — for autonomous dispatch, person-scoring, or fake-report detection. Rules deliver most v1 value.
Is geo-fencing justified? Yes — it is the core mechanism, provided it is on-device, buffered/dwell-gated against GPS error, offline-capable, and governed by a disciplined zone-authoring workflow. Without zone governance it degenerates into noise.
Is blockchain justified? Narrowly and conditionally. Not for identity or location. Defensible solely as tamper-evidence for incident/audit records among genuinely distrusting agencies — and only if those agencies operate nodes; otherwise a signed, hash-chained, publicly anchored log achieves the same trust outcome with a fraction of the complexity. The product requirement is trustworthy records; blockchain is one implementation, not the goal.

1. Technical Executive Summary
Purpose. Close the detection → verified response → trusted record loop for tourists: consent-tiered location monitoring with on-device geo-fencing, missed-check-in/anomaly escalation, context-rich SOS delivered to authority dashboards (and ultimately into state ERSS-112 PSAPs), multi-agency incident timelines, and tamper-evident audit anchoring.
Major technical challenges (ranked).
Mobile background execution — Android OEM battery killers and iOS's refusal to run arbitrary background services make "continuous monitoring" a design negotiation, not an API call (§4).
Offline-first correctness — the highest-risk zones have no network; SOS, fences, and check-ins must work from a local encrypted queue with SMS fallback and exactly-once semantics on sync (§18).
GPS uncertainty vs. geo-fence truth — 3–100 m error means fence events need buffers, dwell timers, and accuracy gates or the system drowns responders in noise (§11).
Emergency-grade reliability on commodity infrastructure — an SOS path with p95 < 5 s and no single point of failure, built by a small team (§21).
Privacy-preserving tracking — DPDP-native consent tiers, retention, and access logging engineered into the data path, not bolted on (§17).
Trust-boundary sprawl — tourists, police, hospitals, tourism dept, admins: five principals with conflicting interests sharing one incident record (§14, §16).
Recommended architectural style [REC]. Modular monolith + event-driven internals (transactional outbox → stream) for MVP and early production; extract high-churn/high-scale modules (Location ingestion, Notification) into services only when metrics force it. Full microservices from day one is rejected (§6, ADR-01).
Major components. Tourist mobile app (Flutter, native background modules), authority web dashboard (Next.js), API gateway + modular backend (FastAPI), PostgreSQL+PostGIS+TimescaleDB-style partitioning, Redis (cache/streams), object storage (S3-compatible), notification fan-out (FCM/APNs + SMS gateway), rules-based risk engine, blockchain adapter (hash-chain + anchoring), observability stack.
Critical engineering risks. Android/iOS killing the monitoring process silently; false-positive anomaly storms destroying responder trust; location-table write amplification; SMS fallback dependency on telecom approvals; ERSS integration being organisationally unavailable during the project window (mock it, design the seam).
MVP recommendation. One FastAPI monolith, one Postgres/PostGIS, Redis, Flutter app with a real Android foreground service, dashboard with WebSocket live updates, rules risk engine, simulated SMS/KYC/ERSS, 2-node Besu (or signed transparency log) anchoring incident-event Merkle roots. Demo must show a real fence breach on a walked route and a real offline SOS syncing on reconnect.
Production recommendation. Same logical modules deployed as 3–5 services (Core, Location-Ingest, Realtime/Notify, Risk-Workers, Blockchain-Adapter) on Kubernetes, Kafka event bus, per-state data partitions, MeitY-empanelled Indian cloud region [ASSUMPTION — government hosting norms], DPDP-compliant retention jobs, HSM-backed keys.

3. Complete End-to-End Workflows
Notation: services in bold; events in code; DB = Postgres write; Q = queue publish; BC = blockchain/anchor write; N = notification.
W1 Registration/Login. App → Auth /auth/register → DB(User) → OTP via Notify (N:SMS) → /auth/verify-otp → DB(Device registered) → tokens. Failure: OTP gateway down → email fallback → both down: retry-after with queue. Idempotency: phone number natural key; verify-otp idempotent on (user, otp-id).
W2 Trip creation & start. App /trips → Trip DB(Trip draft). /trips/{id}/start (Idempotency-Key) → Trip validates consent tier → DB(Trip.active, Consent receipt) → Q:TripStarted → Geofence returns regional zone-pack version → app downloads pack (cache-control, delta if version known) → Risk initialises baseline. Failure: pack download fails → app retries; trip still starts with stale/absent pack (flagged "advisories unavailable").
W3 Background location monitoring. Device buffers fixes → every T (mode-dependent) /locations/batch (batch id = idempotency key) → Location-Ingest validates (monotonic timestamps, speed sanity <250 km/h, accuracy field) → DB bulk insert (partitioned) → cache: Redis trip:{id}:lastfix → Q:LocationUpdated (latest only, not every point) → Risk consumes. Retry: client exponential backoff, batches merge; server dedupes on (device, batch id) and (trip, ts) unique.
W4 Geo-fence entry/exit. On-device engine detects candidate entry → applies accuracy gate (fix accuracy < class threshold), buffer, dwell → local notification; if class ∈ {restricted, disaster}: /geofences/events (event id = client UUID) → Geofence server re-validates against authoritative polygon (PostGIS ST_Contains) & zone version → DB(GeoFenceEvent) → Q:GeoFenceEntered → Risk raises score → Notify authority if policy says so. Exit mirrors with GeoFenceExited, closing dwell. Duplicate suppression: (device, zone, direction) cooldown 5 min + server unique constraint. GPS drift: server rejects events whose fix accuracy radius overlaps boundary by >50% → "uncertain" state, no authority alert.
W5 High-risk detection / anomaly. Risk worker evaluates streams + timers (missed check-in job scans check_in_due < now()) → threshold crossed → DB(RiskAssessment) → Q:RiskLevelChanged → user-challenge: N:push "Are you OK? respond in 10 min" → (a) user OK → deescalate, log; (b) timeout → N:emergency contacts; (c) second timeout or critical context → Incident auto-creates (type=anomaly) → dashboard queue. No automatic dispatch ever (§13).
W6 Manual SOS (online). App composes packet {sos client-UUID, type, fix+accuracy, batteryLvl} → /sos (Idempotency-Key=client UUID) → SOS DB(SOSAlert) + creates Incident atomically (outbox) → Q:SOSTriggered,IncidentCreated → Realtime pushes to dashboard WS ≤1 s → Notify N:emergency contacts (push+SMS) → app receives 202 {sosId} then WS/push status ("received → acknowledged by Unit-12 at 14:03"). Operator ack /sos/{id}/acknowledge → Q:ResponderAcknowledged → app UI. Failure: if no operator ack in 60 s → SOS escalation timer → supervisor queue + N:voice-call bridge [PROD]. Duplicate: same client UUID → same sosId (idempotent); user also dials 112 → operator "link external incident" merge tool.
W7 Silent SOS. Same as W6 with silent=true: no local sounds/UI change; confirmation via single vibration; dashboard flags COVERT (responders instructed: no call-back to victim phone; SMS decoy option).
W8 Offline SOS (critical path). No data network → app: (1) persists SOS to encrypted queue, (2) attempts SMS to shortcode: SOS|v1|<sosUUID>|<lat>|<lon>|<acc>|<ts>|<idRef> (~110 chars, fits one SMS) via silent SMS API where OS allows / user-visible composer otherwise [Android allows programmatic SMS with permission; iOS cannot send SMS programmatically → present pre-filled composer; document this honestly] → SMS gateway → SOS ingests, creates incident (source=sms, unverified-channel flag) → normal dispatch. (3) App keeps radio-scanning; on any connectivity: sync queue → server merges SMS-incident and app-incident by sosUUID (idempotent). Failure: no GSM either → device continues local alarm/strobe; queue persists across reboot; trek checkpoint logic (§11) provides the server-side detection fallback via missed expected sync.
W9 Incident lifecycle & dispatch. States: created → acknowledged → assigned → enroute → on_scene → resolved → closed (+ cancelled, false_alarm, merged). Each transition: /incidents/{id}/status (role-gated per transition matrix) → DB + append IncidentEvent (hash-chained, prevHash) → Q:IncidentStatusChanged → Realtime to app+dashboards → milestone events batched to Blockchain-Adapter (§14). Police dispatch = assign transition; ambulance dispatch = parallel medical assignment creating hospital card (W10).
W10 Ambulance/hospital handoff. Medical incident → Dispatch assigns 108 unit [mock in MVP] → hospital selected → Incident issues scoped medical-access grant (incidentId, hospitalOrg, TTL 24 h) → hospital dashboard card + QR resolve path → access events → AuditLog → treatment updates append to timeline.
W11 Evidence upload. App hashes file (SHA-256) locally → /incidents/{id}/evidence/presign {hash, size, mime} → Evidence returns S3 presigned PUT → app uploads → /evidence/confirm → server re-hashes object, must equal client hash → DB(Evidence) → Q:EvidenceUploaded → Blockchain-Adapter includes hash in next anchor batch → /evidence/{id}/verify recomputes + checks anchor inclusion proof. Failure: hash mismatch → reject, client re-uploads; presign expiry → re-request.
W12 Blockchain anchoring. Blockchain-Adapter consumes IncidentCreated|StatusChanged|EvidenceUploaded → appends to per-incident hash chain (eventHash = H(prevHash‖canonical(event))) in DB → every N minutes/M events builds Merkle tree of new event hashes → submits root via anchorBatch(root, count, fromTs, toTs) → on receipt: DB(BlockchainAnchor{txId, root, proofPaths}) → Q:BlockchainAnchorConfirmed. Failure: chain unreachable → anchors queue (DLQ after retries); system remains fully functional — anchoring is asynchronous and non-blocking by design; gap is visible in verify API ("pending anchor").
W13 Escalation. Triggers: ack SLA breach, severity upgrade, multi-victim, operator action. Incident snapshots state → notifies supervisor role + optionally SDRF org → escalation event appended. Idempotent per (incident, level).
W14 Duplicate incident handling. Dedup keys: same tourist active SOS (client UUID), spatial-temporal cluster (same reporter within 200 m/10 min), SMS+app twin (sosUUID). Server auto-merges twins; operator UI offers merge for clusters → merged state preserves both timelines under primary.
W15 False alarm. Cancel-in-window → cancelled, no dispatch, counters incremented (no punitive action first N). Post-dispatch cancel or operator determination → false_alarm with reason code; repeat pattern (≥3/30 d) → human-review flag only. All still anchored (false alarms are auditable events too).
W16 Network restoration sync. Connectivity regained → client sync order: (1) queued SOS/fence-critical events, (2) location batches oldest-first, (3) media. Server conflict rule: server timeline is authoritative for incident state; client events are inserts, never updates → no true conflicts; late location points accepted if within trip window, marked late_sync=true.

4. Mobile Background Service Architecture (critical)
4.1 Android reality [stable platform behaviour; re-verify current API level]
Permissions ladder: ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION (while-in-use) → separate user step for ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION ("Allow all the time") via settings redirect; Android 12+ users may grant coarse-only — app must detect and degrade. Play Store policy requires declared, in-app-disclosed background-location use; safety apps qualify but expect review friction.
Foreground Service (FGS) with foregroundServiceType="location" and a persistent notification is the only sanctioned way to sample continuously. Android 14+ requires declaring FGS types in manifest and runtime justification; starting FGS from background is restricted (must start while app visible or from allowed triggers: high-priority FCM, alarm, boot receiver).
Doze/App Standby: deep Doze defers network and jobs; FGS with location type is largely exempt from location throttling but network windows still batch; setExactAndAllowWhileIdle alarms for check-in timers; high-priority FCM pierces Doze for the user-challenge flow.
OEM killers (Xiaomi/Oppo/Vivo/OnePlus aggressive task-killers) kill even FGS. Mitigations: battery-optimisation exemption prompt (ACTION_REQUEST_IGNORE_BATTERY_OPTIMIZATIONS — Play-policy-acceptable for safety category [ASSUMPTION — verify policy]), OEM-specific guidance screens (dontkillmyapp-style), server-side liveness watchdog: if a monitored trip's device misses 2× expected sync, that itself is a signal (this converts OS unreliability into a detection feature).
Process death recovery: BOOT_COMPLETED receiver restarts monitoring for active trips; START_STICKY service; WorkManager periodic job (≥15 min) as belt-and-braces re-checker. Force-stop by user = total stop until next app open (OS guarantee) — must be disclosed in UX; server watchdog covers it.
Geofencing API (Google Play services): ~100 fences/app, power-efficient but latency up to minutes and reliant on Fused Location — use it as a wake-up hint, with our own on-device polygon engine as truth (§11).
4.2 iOS reality
No arbitrary background services exist. Options: (a) Standard location updates with allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates=true + "Always" authorization + background-mode location — continuous but battery-visible (blue indicator) and OS may still suspend on memory pressure; (b) Significant-Change Location Service (SLC) — wakes app on ~500 m cell-level changes, survives app termination and reboot; (c) Region monitoring — hardware-assisted, max 20 regions per app, circular only, radius ≥ ~100 m practical; survives termination; (d) Visit monitoring — too coarse. Background App Refresh off / Low Power Mode further constrain (b).
"Always" location requires provisional flow (WhenInUse first, then upgrade prompt) and App Store justification; users can silently downgrade to WhenInUse — app must detect via authorizationStatus and warn.
Push: APNs high-priority alerts wake UI but cannot silently run arbitrary code reliably (content-available background pushes are best-effort, throttled).
SMS cannot be sent programmatically — offline SOS on iOS = pre-filled MFMessageComposeViewController (one user tap) + queue-for-sync. Document honestly.
iPhone satellite SOS/Messages via satellite exist on recent hardware in supported regions — treat as OS-level complement, not an integratable API [UNRESOLVED — India availability].
4.3 “Should the app run as a background service?” — explicit answer
Android: YES — a location-type Foreground Service with persistent notification during active trips only, plus Play-services geofence wake-ups, boot/app-update receivers, WorkManager re-checker, and battery-optimisation exemption for the safety use case. Continuous background monitoring is legitimate and technically supported, with OEM-killer risk mitigated by the server-side missed-sync watchdog.
iOS: NO — not as a persistent service, because the platform forbids it. Instead compose: continuous updates (allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates) only during active trips, SLC as the resurrection mechanism after suspension, the 20-region budget spent dynamically on the nearest/highest-priority fences (re-seeded on each SLC wake), and check-in timers implemented server-side (push-challenge) rather than as local background timers. Accept and disclose: iOS fidelity < Android fidelity; the server watchdog + user-challenge design keeps the safety property (detection of silence) intact even when the app is suspended.
4.4 Monitoring mode engine
Mode transitions are events themselves (logged); parameters are server-versioned config so thresholds tune without app releases.

9. Real-Time Communication
Assignments [REC]: SOS alert to dashboard: WS (+ audible), with server-confirmed display-receipt; incident updates to tourist: push + WS-if-foreground + SMS for critical; live tourist location: app→server via batched HTTPS normally, WS stream in emergency mode; server→dashboard via WS; risk warnings: push (advisory) / push+SMS (critical).

11. Geospatial & Geo-Fencing Architecture
Where does fencing logic execute? [REC] HYBRID — device is the detector, server is the validator and authority-notifier.
Device-side (primary): offline-capable, low-latency, privacy-preserving (advisory-zone hits never leave the device). Implementation: signed regional zone packs (protobuf: zone id, class, version, geometry simplified to ≤200 vertices with ST_SimplifyPreserveTopology, buffer, schedule) cached per trip region; evaluation = bbox prefilter → ray-casting point-in-polygon (or precompiled H3 cell membership sets for very complex polygons [ALT]); OS geofence APIs (Android Play-services fences; iOS's 20 circular regions seeded with nearest/highest-priority zones) used only as low-power wake-up triggers, our engine confirms.
Server-side (secondary): authoritative PostGIS validation of restricted/disaster events (protects against tampered packs and stale versions); periodic sweep of latest fixes vs. newly created temporary zones (a device can't know a fence created after its last pack sync — the server closes that gap and pushes pack-update notifications); analytics.
GPS drift & event quality: accuracy gate (fix.accM must be < class threshold: restricted 30 m, advisory 75 m); boundary hysteresis: entry requires point inside shrunk polygon (buffer −accM), exit requires outside expanded polygon; dwell timers (restricted ≥60 s, advisory ≥30 s); N-consecutive-fix confirmation (≥2); speed sanity (drop teleport fixes >250 km/h implied). Duplicate suppression: per (zone,direction) cooldown 5 min device-side + server unique bucket constraint.
Zone lifecycle: create (draw GeoJSON) → validate (self-intersection, area caps, mandatory expiry for temporary) → approval workflow (class-dependent approver) → publish (version++, pack rebuild, delta push notification to affected active trips) → retire (soft). Every version immutable and audit-linked. Temporary disaster zones bypass the slow approval path via a two-person emergency publish (declarer + confirmer) with auto-expiry ≤72 h [REC].

13. Risk Engine Design
Model [REC]: deterministic, explainable, versioned rules with weighted scoring — ML later, behind the same interface.
score = clamp( Σ w_i · f_i(context), 0, 100 ) with factor examples:
Severity bands: 0–29 LOW (log), 30–54 MODERATE (in-app advisory), 55–74 HIGH (user-challenge, notify contacts on challenge timeout), 75–100 CRITICAL (challenge with short 5-min timeout → incident creation → dashboard). Confidence = data-freshness × fix-quality multiplier; low confidence caps severity at HIGH (never CRITICAL on stale data). Hard rule: the engine can never dispatch responders; it can only create a triaged incident for a human. Weights/thresholds are versioned config (admin UI), every assessment stores engine version + factor vector (explainability & tuning). ML future: replace f_i internals or add a learned factor; the challenge-first state machine is permanent regardless of model quality.

16. Security Architecture
AuthN: OIDC provider (Keycloak [PROD] / library-JWT [MVP]); tourists: phone-OTP + optional password; authority users: username+password+mandatory TOTP MFA, org-admin provisioning only (no self-signup); device registration binds a device keypair (attestation via Play Integrity / App Attest [PROD]) — location batches and SOS are device-signed. Special token: long-lived, narrowly-scoped SOS device token so SOS works even with expired sessions.
AuthZ: RBAC (roles above) + ABAC for the sensitive reads: policy = role ∧ org-jurisdiction ∧ (open incident grant ∨ break-glass). Example: operator may read location-history(trip) iff exists incident(trip) ∧ incident.jurisdiction = operator.org ∧ incident.state ∈ open ∧ purpose supplied. Enforced in a single policy module (OPA [ALT] / in-code policy layer [MVP]) — never scattered per-endpoint.
Break-glass: requires target ref + declared reason + second-person approval (or post-hoc review within 24 h if solo at 3 a.m.) → time-boxed grant (≤4 h) → every read logged → tourist notified after incident closure.
Crypto: TLS 1.3 everywhere; mTLS service↔service and partner links; AES-256-GCM at rest (disk + field-level via envelope encryption for id_ref, contacts, medical via KMS data keys); secrets in Vault/cloud secret manager, never env-committed; key rotation: data keys 90 d, signing/anchor keys 1 y with overlap; JWT signing keys via JWKS rotation.
API protection: gateway rate limits (per token + per IP), request-size caps, JSON schema validation, WAF rules, OTP/login lockouts with exponential delay, audit on all admin routes.

17. Privacy Architecture
Engineered controls (DPDP-native, per domain report):
Consent as data: consent receipts (tier, purposes, ts, signature) are first-class rows; every processing job checks tier before touching location data; withdrawal → immediate pipeline stop + retention clock start.
Tiers in the data path: CHECKIN_ONLY → no location rows at all (only check-in events); GEOFENCE_ALERTS → advisory evaluation on-device, server stores no advisory events, only restricted/disaster; FULL → batched trail with retention. Default = least invasive.
Minimisation: adaptive sampling already minimises; server stores accuracy-truncated coords for analytics (3 decimals ≈ 110 m) separately from operational precise fixes; aggregates use H3 cells with k≥20 suppression.
Purpose limitation in code: location read APIs require purpose enum + incident grant; analytics reads only the truncated store; no export API for raw trails.
Retention as jobs: partition-drop schedules per §10; deletion certificates logged; user-initiated deletion API honours legal holds (open incidents) transparently.
Pseudonymisation: analytics pipeline re-keys user ids per-epoch; dashboards show credential ref, full PII on click-through with audit.
Emergency override: break-glass §16 — bounded, logged, notified.
Anti-surveillance guarantees (product commitments): no standing tracking outside active trips; no advisory-zone telemetry; no cross-trip movement profiling; access statistics published; independent audit hooks. The honest answer to "how does this avoid mass surveillance": by making the invasive tiers opt-in, the invisible tiers default, the sensitive reads incident-gated, and every read auditable — and by not building the features (standing tracking, face recognition, cross-referencing) that convert a safety tool into a surveillance tool.

18. Offline & Poor-Network Architecture
Sync semantics: store-and-forward with per-item idempotency keys; oldest-first within lane; duplicates impossible at server via unique keys; conflicts structurally avoided (client emits immutable events, never updates shared state); late_sync flag preserves analytic honesty. Exponential backoff everywhere with full jitter; retry budgets per lane so media never starves SOS.

19. Notification Architecture
Design: template registry with pre-translated strings for every critical message (no live MT on critical path); per-channel providers behind adapter (FCM, APNs, SMS aggregator, TTS/voice); per-channel circuit breakers + health probes; delivery-receipt webhook ingestion normalises statuses; dedupe collapse keys so a user gets one coherent alert, not five channels shouting; quiet-hours respected for ≤WARNING only.

20. Scalability Research
Assumptions: 5% of registered users on an active trip at any time; Active-Trip mode ≈ 1 fix/60 s, batched every 7 min (~7 points/batch); dashboards ≈ 1 WS per 2k users; incidents ≈ 0.5% of active trips/day.
Levers [REC]: Redis last-fix + zone-pack CDN caching (reads never hit PG hot path); monthly partitions + BRIN/ts indexes; batch inserts (COPY) in Location-Ingest; Redis Streams → Kafka at ~100k users; horizontal API replicas behind LB (stateless); rate limits per device with server-driven nextSyncHintSec for load-shedding by slowing sampling globally during spikes (grace degradation nobody notices); notification queue with priority lanes. Single beefy Postgres + replica comfortably serves 1M-user profile above; sharding is a >5M-user conversation.

21. Reliability & Failure Engineering
Defaults: client timeout 10 s (SOS 5 s), service-to-service 3 s; retries 3× exp+jitter on idempotent ops only; circuit breakers (50% error over 20 req → open 30 s) per dependency; DLQs on every consumer with replay tooling; idempotency per §7/§15; health: liveness (process) + readiness (DB, Redis, bus) endpoints; graceful degradation ladder: lose analytics → lose anchoring → lose advisory pushes → never lose SOS intake (isolated pool, minimal deps: LB → SOS module → PG-or-fallback-log).
Backups/DR: PG PITR (WAL archiving, 5-min RPO), nightly base backups, cross-region copy [PROD]; S3 versioning+replication; Redis is rebuildable cache (persistence AOF for streams); RTO targets: core API 30 min, dashboards 30 min, anchoring 24 h. 28. Architecture Decision Records
ADR-01 Modular monolith vs microservices. Context: 4–8 devs, emergency-grade reliability, unknown scale. Options: monolith, modular monolith+events, microservices. Decision: modular monolith + outbox events. Reason: consistency-critical incident core benefits from local transactions; team too small for distributed ops; extraction path preserved. Consequences: single blast radius → mitigate with replicas; must police module boundaries in review.
ADR-02 REST vs GraphQL. Options: REST+OpenAPI, GraphQL. Decision: REST. Reason: idempotency keys, HTTP caching for packs, simpler rate-limiting/authZ per route, dashboard needs are predictable; GraphQL's flexible queries complicate ABAC on sensitive fields. Consequence: a few aggregate endpoints hand-built.
ADR-03 WebSocket vs SSE. Decision: WS for dashboards + emergency app mode; polling fallback; SSE rejected for lack of upstream channel (operator acks ride the socket) though acceptable fallback. Consequence: sticky sessions + Redis pub/sub required.
ADR-04 PostgreSQL/PostGIS. Decision: adopt — single engine covers relational, geospatial, JSONB, partitioning; boring, hireable, free. Consequence: time-series volume handled by partitioning discipline (or TimescaleDB later).
ADR-05 Redis. Decision: adopt for last-fix cache, sessions, rate limits, Streams (MVP bus), WS pub/sub. Consequence: treat as rebuildable except Streams (AOF on); migrate bus role to Kafka at scale.
ADR-06 Queue: Redis Streams → Kafka. Decision: Streams for MVP, Kafka at ~100k users (retention/replay/consumer-group needs). RabbitMQ rejected: no long replay log. Consequence: envelope + consumer idempotency designed transport-agnostic from day one.
ADR-07 Client vs server geofencing. Decision: hybrid — device detects, server validates restricted/disaster + sweeps new temp zones. Reason: offline requirement makes device-side mandatory; server closes stale-pack and tamper gaps. Consequence: parity test suite between the two engines is permanent CI.
ADR-08 Rules vs ML. Decision: deterministic versioned rules for risk/anomaly v1; hosted-API AI only for translation + assistive triage; ML behind same interface later. Reason: no training data exists; explainability for emergencies; FP cost is trust-fatal. Consequence: invest in config tooling + assessment logging to bootstrap future labels.
ADR-09 Public vs permissioned blockchain. Decision: permissioned Besu consortium, batched Merkle-root anchoring only; transparency-log fallback switch; optional public root mirror. Reason: §14 — governance fit, cost, privacy; roots leak nothing. Consequence: consortium MoU is a launch dependency for the "decentralised" claim, not for product function.
ADR-10 On-chain vs off-chain. Decision: on-chain = batch roots + signatures only; everything else off-chain encrypted. Reason: DPDP erasure vs immutability, honeypot avoidance, cost, metadata leakage. Consequence: verification requires our proof store — mitigated by giving tourists/oversight downloadable proofs.
